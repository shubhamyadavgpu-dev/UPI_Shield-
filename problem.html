The hidden payment-security gap

The user can be real. The payment can still be fraudulent.

Modern payment authentication can successfully verify the person making a transaction while completely missing the fact that the person has been manipulated into making it.

The real-world problem

Scams don't always break the system. They manipulate the user.

Social-engineering attacks exploit human decision making. The victim may have a valid device, a valid account and a valid PIN — yet still authorize a fraudulent transaction.

⚡

Fake urgency

"Pay within 10 minutes or your electricity, SIM or bank account will be blocked."

☎

Impersonation

Attackers pretend to be bank employees, police officers, delivery agents or officials.

!

Coercion

Fear, threats and psychological pressure can force users to make decisions they normally would not make.

Authentication gap

2FA can verify who. It doesn't always understand why.

A PIN, OTP or biometric can prove that the legitimate account holder approved a transaction. But authentication alone cannot determine whether the approval happened because of deception or coercion.

The required shift

Authentication is only one part of trust.

Payment security needs to understand the context surrounding a transaction — not just the credentials used to approve it.

Traditional approach

  • × Is the account legitimate?
  • × Is the PIN correct?
  • × Is the OTP valid?
  • × Authorization completed.

Contextual approach

  • ✓ What triggered this payment?
  • ✓ Is there unusual urgency?
  • ✓ Is the message manipulative?
  • ✓ Does the transaction context make sense?
